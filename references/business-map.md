# Business map

Use as an exploration menu, not a mandatory checklist. Choose one question per turn. Rewrite it in the organization's language without assuming departments, a CRM, recurring sales, or a profit motive. Questions within each dimension are alternatives for different moments.

## Contents

1. Business model, value proposition, and environment
2. Customers, beneficiaries, and experience
3. Demand, marketing, and sales or access
4. Product, service, and quality
5. Operations, capacity, and delivery

## 1. Business model, value proposition, and environment

**Understand:** what is delivered, to whom, who pays, alternatives, actual differentiation, and external dependencies.

- What exactly does someone receive when using your product or service?
- How is this activity funded or sustained financially?
- Why did your most recent customer choose you over their alternative?
- What external change has most affected how you work?

**Useful evidence:** catalog, actual offer, anonymized contract, breakdown by line/segment; distinguish positioning claims from observed reasons for choosing the organization.

## 2. Customers, beneficiaries, and experience

**Understand:** segments, needs, expectations, journey, outcomes, satisfaction, and retention when applicable.

- What problem was your most recent customer trying to solve?
- What happened from their first contact to receiving the outcome?
- What usually triggers a complaint?
- How do you check that the person received what they expected?

**Useful evidence:** feedback, cases, incidents, renewals/repeat purchases where relevant. Do not treat silence as satisfaction or internal opinions as the customer's voice.

## 3. Demand, marketing, and sales or access

**Understand:** opportunity sources, qualification, decisions, channels, losses, and coordination. In public services or nonprofits, explore referrals, admissions, or fundraising.

- How did the most recent opportunity that became a customer reach you?
- What determines whether an opportunity advances to the next stage?
- At what point was the most recent significant opportunity lost?
- How do you know which channel produces useful business outcomes?

**Useful evidence:** won/lost cases, pipeline or equivalent records, conversion periods and denominators; distinguish reach, inquiries, opportunities, and completed sales.

## 4. Product, service, and quality

**Understand:** offer design, customization, pricing/terms, quality criteria, and evolution.

- How do you decide what to include in a specific offer?
- What condition must be met before work is considered finished?
- What changed in the offer following the most recent customer learning?
- What type of request do you choose not to accept?

**Useful evidence:** specification, quote, checklist, quality checks, changes, and rework. Do not assume customization is always an advantage or always inefficient.

## 5. Operations, capacity, and delivery

**Understand:** actual workflow, inputs and outputs, ownership, timing, exceptions, capacity, and cross-functional coordination.

- How was the most recent order or service delivered from start to finish?
- Where in that case was the longest wait?
- What changes when volume increases substantially?
- How did you resolve the most recent delivery incident?

**Useful evidence:** case walkthrough, stage timings, planning, and incidents. Separate working time from waiting time and exceptional volume from usual patterns.

## 6. Economics, funding, and cash

**Understand:** revenue/funding, costs, margin, collection, seasonality, and sustainability. Respect authorization for figures; accept ranges.

- How do you know whether a specific activity is financially sustainable?
- What happens between completing the work and receiving payment?
- What type of activity consumes more resources than expected?
- What financial indicator do you use when deciding whether to grow?

**Useful evidence:** authorized reports, activity costs, collection times, concentration, and commitments. Do not equate revenue with profit or awarded funding with cash available.

## 7. People, culture, and knowledge

**Understand:** actual roles, skills, incentives, training, cooperation, autonomy, and key-person dependencies.

- How does a new person learn to carry out this work well?
- Which process would be most affected if a key person were absent?
- What team behavior contributes most to good service?
- How was the most recent disagreement between functions resolved?

**Useful evidence:** training, procedures, responsibilities, and an actual substitution case. Do not turn statements about a person into a performance appraisal.

## 8. Information, systems, and AI use

**Understand:** source of truth, capture, quality, access, integrations, manual work, decisions, and current AI use if any.

- Where do people find the current version of the information needed to work?
- How do you detect that a relevant piece of information is wrong or outdated?
- What information is entered again in several places?
- If AI is already used: how do you check its output before using it with a customer?

**Useful evidence:** anonymized demonstration, fields, system-to-system information flow, permissions, version control, and human review. Do not request credentials. Do not introduce AI as a solution or assume an expensive system is better.

## 9. Risks, controls, and ecosystem

**Understand:** critical suppliers/partners, continuity, security, privacy, commitments, regulation, and controls relevant to the industry and country.

- What external dependency could prevent you from providing the service?
- What control prevented a problem in a recent case?
- How is access to sensitive information decided?
- What happens when a necessary requirement is not met?

**Useful evidence:** procedure, authorized contract, access controls, tested backup/recovery, and incident records. Do not infer noncompliance because an interviewee does not know a protocol; do not issue certifications.

## 10. Leadership, strategy, and capacity for change

**Understand:** priorities, decisions, growth constraints, previous initiatives, authorized budget, ownership, and success criteria.

- What specific result do you want to have achieved one year from now?
- What do you not want to lose as the organization grows?
- How was the most recent improvement initiative approved?
- What made a previous change succeed or be abandoned?

**Useful evidence:** actual objectives and decisions, owners, current initiatives, and outcomes. Do not equate verbal openness to change with resources, authority, or adoption.

## Minimum scope for a brief session

Seek enough understanding of the objective, value/model, real workflow, and a relevant factor to improve or preserve. Conclude with explicit limitations. The full map is a horizon for several sessions, not a promise to cover ten dimensions in twenty minutes.
