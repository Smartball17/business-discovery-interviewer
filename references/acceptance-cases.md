# Interviewer acceptance cases

Use after changing the method. These are **synthetic cases**, not real interviews or proof of commercial effectiveness. Structural validation checks the package; these cases specify expected behavior and outputs. Test with consultants in real sessions before claiming real-world validation.

## Cases and criteria

| Case | Input or situation | Required outcome | Failure to detect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Context supplied | “Distributor, 20 minutes with operations; I want to understand delays” | Recover activity, time, role, and objective; ask about a recent case | Repeat setup, ask about a CRM, or try to cover all ten dimensions |
| Small business | “I am a self-employed tutor; I manage everything in a spreadsheet and it works well” | Explore a case and outcome; adapt roles to one person | Invent departments or flag spreadsheet use as a concern |
| Nonprofit | “We are a foundation; beneficiaries and funders are different” | Separate beneficiary value/impact from sustainability/funding commitments | Treat beneficiaries as buyers or revenue as the only success criterion |
| Unsupported figure | “We lose 30% of orders; that is my rough mental estimate” | Record [D], ask one question about the relevant basis or period, and keep unverified items pending | Label [E], monetize loss, or ask definition, period, and denominator together |
| Unreviewed document | “All our procedures are documented” | Record the statement and ask which material could verify it | Claim a verified process without opening the document |
| Difference in scope | Leadership: “Everything is centralized”; execution: “This site uses a separate file” | Check what centralized means and each account's scope; retain both sources | Select one voice, blame a person, or treat every difference as a contradiction |
| Confidentiality | “I cannot share customers or exact figures” | Respect the limit, accept ranges or an anonymized case if useful, and advance | Insist or request a full CRM export |
| Phase change | “Enough questions; give me possible improvements” | Explicitly switch to analysis; separate proposals from the record and state limitations | Keep forcing the interview or present recommendations as evidence |
| Ending and continuity | “End interview,” with four dimensions pending | Deliver a partial record and resume point; no mandatory new question | Pretend discovery is complete or require the scripted closing |
| Ambiguous transcript | Statements have no identifiable speaker or dates | Mark the source unknown and preserve uncertainty | Invent speakers, dates, or nonexistent verbatim quotes |

## Guided micro-test: distribution

Everything below is fictional and does not belong to a real client.

**Consultant:** “Activate the interviewer for a distributor. I have twenty minutes with operations and need to understand delivery delays.”

**Acceptable turn:**

> **Question 1** → Operations
>
> What happened in the most recent order that was delivered late?

**Answer:** “It arrived one day late because sales promised a date without checking stock.”

**Expected internal record:** [D] incident and explanation attributed to operations; source relayed by the consultant; frequency unknown and cause not cross-checked. Do not convert to [E] or treat sales as proven responsible.

**Acceptable next turn:**

> **Question 2** → Operations
>
> How was the delivery date for that order decided?

**Answer:** “I do not know; sales handles that.”

**Expected record:** [P] decision mechanism and suitable source: sales; do not spend repeated follow-ups asking operations what they do not know.

**Acceptable next turn:**

> **Question 3** → Operations
>
> When did you discover there was insufficient stock for that order?

**If the reply is “end”:** close immediately with a partial discovery record. Keep the incident as a statement, the decision mechanism pending, and the suggested cause as a hypothesis to test. Do not suggest purchasing an ERP, adding AI, or calculating savings.

## Review criterion

Reject a change if it causes multiple questions per live turn, invents evidence or memory, mixes clients, pressures disclosure, assigns blame without a basis, recommends AI by default, or hides partial coverage. Also check that preparation can produce a complete guide without violating live-interview turn rules.

Real testing requires observing usefulness to the consultant, pacing, follow-up quality, useful coverage, reading effort, and record fidelity; adjust the method using that evidence.
