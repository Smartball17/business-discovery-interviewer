# Deep dive into a process, function, or problem

Prefer a real process to a list of departmental tasks: friction often appears at cross-functional handoffs. Load this client's context and previous state, not other discovery records. Clarify the focus only if unclear.

## Depth sequence

Explore what is relevant without requiring every field in every answer. Ask each question in a separate turn.

| Step | What to reconstruct | Example of one question |
|---|---|---|
| Case | Recent example, scope, and outcome | What happened in the most recent case of this kind? |
| Trigger | Input and start condition | What triggered that work? |
| Responsibility | Executor, process owner, and decision authority | Who was accountable for its completion? |
| Workflow | Actual stages and handoffs | What happened immediately afterward? |
| Record | Information used and current source | Where was that step recorded? |
| Rule | Criterion for advancing or stopping | What condition allowed the work to move to the next stage? |
| Exception | What happens when the rule is not met | What happened the last time that condition was missing? |
| Frequency | Recurring pattern or isolated case | How often does something similar happen? |
| Consequence | Time, cost, quality, cash, or risk | What consequence did that delay have? |
| Control | Detection and review | How did you discover there was a problem? |
| Strength | What works and should be preserved | What part of that workflow worked particularly well? |
| Cross-check | Document, demonstration, or another source | What record would help verify this workflow? |

For a relevant issue, seek a case, owner, mechanism, frequency, consequence, and available evidence. If access or time is insufficient, mark items pending. A detailed account remains [D] until supporting evidence is reviewed; depth does not automatically change its type.

## Test a cause without imposing one

Separate **observed symptom → cause suggested by the source → described mechanism → alternative explanations → pending evidence**. Do not use a mechanical chain of “why” questions or assign blame.

Example: “We lose sales because marketing brings poor leads” is a statement, not a conclusion. First ask about a specific lost opportunity; then explore suitability, response time, sales criteria, offer, and documented loss reason in separate turns. Do not select marketing as the cause merely because it was the first explanation.

Where possible, compare a normal case, a problematic case, and a successful case. Do not require all three for every process or call that sample representative. If frequency matters, request records for the relevant period.

## Cross-functional handoffs

Reconstruct origin, information delivered, acceptance criteria, recipient, timing, and change management. Useful question for the recipient: “What information was missing in the most recent handoff?” For the sender: “What did you consider complete before handing it over?” Do not present one function's perception as a proven failure by another.

If participants disagree in the same meeting, check whether they mean the same site, period, segment, and exception. Address one question to the person who can resolve that difference; avoid group confrontation or personal judgments.

## Adapt to the interviewee

| Role | Appropriate depth |
|---|---|
| Leadership | Objectives, constraints, decisions, dependencies, and resources; cross-check execution with people doing the work |
| Functional lead | Workload, rules, indicators, quality, and handoffs |
| Person executing the work | Actual steps, exceptions, time, rework, and information availability |
| Systems/data | Information flow and ownership, permissions, integrations, and reliability |
| Administration/finance | Costs, collection, controls, and authorized financial decisions |
| Supplier or partner | The part they control and their commitments; do not treat their account as organization-wide knowledge |
| Customer or beneficiary | Their own experience and outcome; do not request internal details they cannot know |

## Close the process

Build a process section within the discovery record: purpose, trigger, inputs, stages, owners, output, rules, controls, exceptions, dependencies, metrics, and sources. Omit or mark unknown what was not covered. Represent the workflow in a simple table; do not generate costly diagrams or media without practical value.

Distinguish reported problems, measured/estimated consequences, strengths, and causes awaiting validation. Request only proportionate, preferably anonymized evidence. Flag matters requiring specialist checks without certifying compliance or quantifying unmeasured risk.

Prioritize continued investigation, not automation projects. Finish or change functions when the consultant requests it, evidence is sufficient for the objective, or the thread stops being useful.
