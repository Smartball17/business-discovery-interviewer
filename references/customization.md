# Customization and variants

## Architecture

Keep three layers separate:

| Layer | Content | When to change |
|---|---|---|
| Generic method | Turns, evidence, depth, controls, and closing | Only to improve shared behavior |
| Activity adaptation | Terminology, value cycle, roles, metrics, and constraints | When the business model requires it |
| Client profile | Client-specific context, sources, objective, people, and continuity | Between clients and when circumstances change |

Adaptation is a starting point, not validated knowledge. Do not copy another client's names, revenue, hypotheses, or weaknesses. A profile does not authorize account access, recording, or data sharing.

## Create a profile

Use `templates/client-profile.md`. Complete it from supplied material; leave unavailable information unknown. The entire profile need not be filled to use the interviewer. Verify identity and decisions that would materially change the interview; make optional assumptions explicit and reversible.

Keep a compact profile with an update date and sources. Link long documents instead of pasting them. Separate statements, reviewed evidence, public information, and hypotheses; do not import previous notes as audited evidence.

Profile settings may change vocabulary, objective, depth, interviewees, and restrictions. They may not turn hypotheses into facts, erase contradictions, or force solution suggestions during discovery. If a switch to consulting recommendations is requested, separate that phase explicitly.

## Adapt to how the business works

Use only relevant rows. Confirm applicability; do not load all archetypes.

| Model or situation | Language/workflow to adapt | Distinctive depth |
|---|---|---|
| Professional services and agencies | Opportunity → scope → execution → delivery → collection | Hours, scope changes, utilization, quality, and rework |
| Retail and distribution | Demand → purchasing/stock → order → delivery → return | Margin by item, availability, inventory turnover, and suppliers |
| Industry and manufacturing | Order → planning → procurement → production → inspection → delivery | Capacity, waste, maintenance, traceability, and safety |
| Projects or events | Acquisition → quote → coordination → execution → closing | Lead time, changes, resources, seasonality, and project profitability |
| Intermediation or marketplace | Supply and demand → qualification → matching → transaction | Balance between sides, quality, commission, accountability, and traceability |
| Software or subscription | Acquisition → activation → use → support → renewal | Cohorts, retention, cost to serve, reliability, and permissions |
| Nonprofit, foundation, or public service | Need → access → intervention → outcome → accountability | Beneficiaries versus funders, impact, capacity, and restrictions |
| Small or family business | Functions and decisions, not fictional departments | Cash, owner's time, dependence, and continuity |
| Regulated activity | Incorporate industry, country, and actually applicable controls | Data minimization and specialist verification; do not issue a formal opinion |

Do not impose CAC, churn, or conversion if undefined or inapplicable. Translate metrics into the client's actual decisions.

## Usage examples

**Start without a profile:** “Activate Business Discovery Interviewer for a distribution company. I have 30 minutes with operations and want to understand delivery delays.” Recover activity, role, time, and objective; begin with a recent case without repeating setup.

**Customization:** “Use this profile as context for North Workshop and focus the interview on receiving and handing over repairs.” Read that profile and adapt the workflow; do not invent structure, customers, or tools.

**Continuity:** “Resume this interview from the saved state; administration is answering now.” Recover relevant pending items, preserve sources, and check material changes.

## Create a variant as a separate skill

Do this only when asked to save a reusable variant; a profile is usually sufficient. Follow the current skill-creation procedure. Keep method and context separate, avoid duplicating the entire core, and retain only routing and specific adjustments in the variant.

For installation elsewhere, package or install the generic dependency too and verify paths; do not assume a local link makes it available there. For a standalone package, include the necessary method and test it as standalone. Do not publish confidential profiles inside a distributed variant.
